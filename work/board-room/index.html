About the eight names

The advisors are simulations. Each one is built only from a well-known person's public work, such as books, shareholder letters, essays, talks and interviews, to show how that kind of thinker weighs a decision. They are not those people, and they do not speak for them.

None of the eight is connected with Indica Technology or with the Board Room, and none of them has reviewed, approved or endorsed it. Their names are used only to say whose public work a seat is built from. Anything a seat says on this page is the simulation's words, never the real person's.

01 / What goes in

One decision, in plain words.

What the board needs

  • The decision, in one line.
  • Your options: two to four. "Keep doing what I do now" counts.
  • Your numbers: revenue, cash and team size.
  • Your limits: deadline, hours and budget.
  • What success looks like, and by when.
  • Which way you lean, and why. The board tests your lean instead of agreeing with it.

Two ways to meet

Full: all eight advisors and three rounds: a first take each, a debate on the points that split them, then a vote on a written plan.

Quick: four advisors picked for the decision. The vote round runs only if they still disagree, or one of them sees a risk of ruin.

Why the board landed here

  • A failed pilot with missing audit logs, fallback, and QA is a one-way door, reputation loss with this bank and future banks is irreversible because enterprise AI reliability is table stakes, not differentiating. Wickman seat
  • The £60k pilot is purely a reference; its standalone value is negligible versus the £180k+ follow-on pipeline, so losing it destroys the whole business model. Buffett seat
  • A six-week delay can be framed as competence if led with the bank’s risk (e.g., audit integrity), not your own unpreparedness, and only if backed by internal QA pass rates ≥99.9% and a camcorder-style plan showing ownership. Thiel seat
  • Founder time is the scarcest capital, shipping a shaky demo guarantees fire drills, not learning; rebuilding now buys back 6x founder hours at £62.50/hour versus £150+ later. Martell seat
  • In enterprise AI, no one checks output, so if the first demo fails audit or fallback, the bank assumes all output is garbage, and judgment errors are costly and non-retryable. Thiel seat

The minority report

“A demo that breaks under audit isn’t just bad—it’s zero users. You have to make something people want.”

Graham seat, outvoted

For this view to be right: That the bank will tolerate a broken demo if fixes are fast and visible, this is false in enterprise AI where audit/fallback are table stakes, not features.

Conditions

  1. Bank signs a written addendum accepting a six-week delay only if production-grade audit logs, fallback, and QA pass rates are delivered before go-live, and the £60k fee is not voided or penalized.
  2. Internal QA passes at 99.9% on answer quality, audit, and fallback recovery by week 4, tested against real bank data (not synthetic).
  3. Founder records and shares a camcorder-style plan (per Martell) with one number (99.9% QA pass rate), outcome, and handoff to lead engineer, with lead engineer present, before calling the bank.
  4. If internal QA pass rate <99% by week 3, pause the ask and rebuild or walk.
  5. Founder confirms no penalty clauses bind the startup for delay.
  6. If bank refuses written confirmation before week 1 ends, walk away.

What to do next

  • Day 1 Founder and Integrator (or lead engineer) sign off on rebuild plan with weekly QA scorecard (e.g., QA pass rate) and camcorder-style plan recorded with lead engineer present.
  • Day 2–3 Ship rebuilt system to internal QA suite; run full test suite against real bank data; capture pass rates; if <95%, pivot to Option C or reshape.
  • If ≥95% by week 2, Day 3 Call bank’s technical lead and project lead separately with same ask framed as 'quality assurance', lead with bank’s audit risk, not your own.
  • If bank confirms in writing, Day 4 Lock addendum with 99.9% QA threshold and production-grade requirement.
  • Week 4 Rebuild complete; QA sign-off recorded; lead engineer certifies system readiness.
Cheapest test
Run a full internal QA pass on audit/fallback/answer quality by week 2; if pass rate <95%, pivot to Option C.
Kill criteria
If internal QA pass rate <95% by week 2, or bank refuses written confirmation before week 1 ends, pivot to Option C.
Revisit
Revisit if bank delays beyond six weeks or insists on 'hardening during pilot', then pivot to Option A only if QA pass rate ≥99.9% by week 2 and bank signs addendum waiving penalties.

04 / What a founder gets

Why it is worth five minutes.

01

Eight opinions without booking eight calls

Strategy, product, pricing, marketing, people, operations, money and competition, in about four and a half minutes for the full board.

02

It argues with your lean

The board tests the option you favour instead of agreeing with it. Above, the founder leaned towards A, and the board scored A 2.3 out of 10.

03

Disagreement, kept on purpose

Every answer ends with the strongest losing view, in that advisor's words, and what would have to be true for it to be right.

04

Numbers you can check

Each option's score is worked out by the software from the advisors' own scores. The model writes the words. It never sets a score.

05

A plan, not an opinion

Conditions, dated next steps, the cheapest test and the point at which to walk away, so you find out early if the call was wrong.

06

Private, with no bill per meeting

It runs on one machine on a local model, so the decision never leaves it. Record what you decided, and bring it back to the board when it is time to review.

06 / Trade-offs

What it is not.

  • It advises. It does not decide. The founder makes the call, and every answer says so.
  • The advisors are simulations. They can be wrong about how the real person would think.
  • A local model is slower and rougher than the best hosted ones. It sometimes breaks one of the board's own rules, which is why the benchmark counts misses.
  • One meeting at a time, on one machine. It is built for a founder, not a team of fifty.
  • It only knows what you tell it. Leave out a number and the board has to assume it, and it lists what it assumed.
