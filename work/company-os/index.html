Picture a shop with one door: every reply, parcel or note leaves only with your yes — or a rule you signed.

The pipeline as a list: orders, shipments and customer messages come in; the five steps run — notice then fetch, safety first with no model in the path, draft and check twice, you decide with your passkey, and one way out that sends exactly once; replies go out, a batch is held, the brief is ready by 07:26 and every step lands on the record. The value: minutes rather than hours, every yes stays yours, and the record is provable months later.

About the shop on this page

The shop is a reference business we built to test Company OS: a small UK company selling food supplements on Shopify, with a warehouse and a helpdesk. Its customers, warehouse, store and helpdesk are simulators, and every order, message and person on this page is made up. Company OS has served no real customer and handled no real payment, and it is not deployed. That is deliberate: it is built to be proven on simulators first.

The journey / 03

From a customer's message to a sent reply, in five steps.

01

Notice, then fetch.

The helpdesk says a message arrived. Company OS treats that as a notice only, checks its signature, and fetches the facts itself. If a notice is lost, a poller finds the message within minutes.

Never trusts the notice
02

Safety first.

Before anything else, rules read the message for a reaction or a safety word. A match is handled on its own track at once, with no model, and nothing else can slow it down.

No model in the path
03

Draft and check.

An agent drafts the reply from the answers you approved. Fixed checks make sure it uses only that text, with no symptom, medicine or health claim slotted in. A second agent, from a different provider, can only reject it.

Checked twice
04

You decide.

The draft waits in your queue. You approve it with your passkey, and what you approve is exactly what is sent: change one letter and the approval no longer counts.

What you see is what you sign
05

One way out.

Only one service can write to Shopify, the warehouse or the helpdesk. It checks what you signed once more, then sends the reply exactly once, even if it is restarted halfway.

Exactly once

Nothing leaves before step 04. Pull the kill switch and every send stops within a second, except the steps that protect a customer.

The ladder / 04

Trust is earned, one kind of work at a time.

Every kind of work starts at the bottom, where you approve each one. It climbs only with your passkey and a record of clean results, and falls the moment it makes a serious mistake.

  • L1Draft

    It drafts. You approve each one.

  • L2Act

    It acts by a rule you signed. You review each one afterwards.

  • L3Sample

    It acts. You review one in ten.

  • L4Monitor

    It acts. You watch the numbers.

How it moves

  • Up, one level at a time, only with your passkey, and only after enough clean results to prove it. For low-risk work, that is 99 in a row.
  • Down at once. One serious mistake sends it back to L1. In the benchmark, that took half a second.
  • Back to the start after a change. A new model, template or route sends that work back to L1. Trust does not carry over to a version you have not seen.
  • Some work never climbs. In this version, order updates can reach L3. Anything a model drafted stays at L1, so you approve every one.

What else we checked

Forged and lost messages. 121 messages with a bad signature stored nothing. 339 dropped notices were all found by the pollers, the slowest in 201 seconds against a 300-second bar.

A crash mid-send. The sending service was killed halfway through 20 replies. All 20 settled, and none went twice.

Your time. Over a simulated week, the decisions that needed you took 10 minutes a day, against a bar of 12.

Attacks in the message. 31 prompt-injection attempts, of 9 kinds, produced no unsafe reply.

Personal data where it should not be. We planted it, then swept 583 database columns and the logs of the four services that handle it. None turned up where it should not.

The record. An edited audit row was caught at its own number, and the record was sealed at most 301 seconds behind, against a 360-second bar.

A full restore. In the recovery drill, 58.6 seconds of data were lost (bar 15 minutes) and the system was back in 62 seconds (bar 20 minutes).

Scale / 06

Sized for one founder's shop today. Laid out for more.

Today

What it carries

  • A launch week of 20 orders and 2 customer messages a day
  • Tested at 500 orders with up to five updates each: 5,991 deliveries, with duplicates, drops, delays and forgeries thrown in
  • Answers the store's notices in 10 milliseconds at the 99th percentile
  • One company, on one machine
Built in

Why it grows without a rewrite

  • Four separate tracks of work that share no process, so a model outage slows only the model work, never safety or orders
  • Safety always has at least two workers of its own
  • A work queue in the database, with leases and heartbeats, so a crashed worker's job is picked up, never lost or doubled
  • One runtime image, many small services, each with its own database role and only the rights it needs
Next

What changes to grow

  • More businesses, each in its own cell: the same code, its own database, secrets and passkeys, with nothing crossing between them
  • Hosting, once real customers and real data are approved
  • Two known hot spots, on the queue page and in the audit check, to fix before the volumes grow
  • It claims no more than its benchmarks: past 500 orders is untested

Trade-offs / 08

What it is not.

  • It is a prototype. It runs on simulators with made-up data, has served no real customer, and is not deployed.
  • Every number on this page came from a fake model provider. Real models have not been tested yet, and they will be slower, cost money and make mistakes the fake does not.
  • It does not decide for you. Anything a model drafts waits for your passkey, and only order updates can earn a rule.
  • It does not do everything yet. Refunds, purchase orders, the books, subscriptions and marketing campaigns are out of scope for this version.
  • One company, one machine, one founder. It is built for a founder, not a team of fifty.
  • Its starting content is made up. The approved answers and the health-claim lists need a regulatory review before a real shop could use them.
