The business case · Company OS

What an agent-run shop is worth to the person who still says yes.

Founders do not buy AI to watch it work — they buy the week it gives back, the proof it stands behind, and the record it keeps. Here is all three, with every number measured on simulators rather than promised.

≈ 50 founder-minutes in a measured launch week 14 of 14 benchmarks passed 19 kinds of events on the record Runs on one machine
01 · Your time

The question is not “can it run the shop?” It is “how much of my week does the shop take?”

The business plan behind Company OS gives a one-person company a perimeter of twelve founder-hours a week. The operating layer is measured against that perimeter, not against a demo.

04 · The audit log of everything

Months later, anyone can ask “what happened?” — and get an answer that can’t be edited.

Every outside action — and the decisions, checks, keys and switches behind it — becomes a row in a chained, sealed, write-once record.

Tamper-evidence is tested, not asserted. In the benchmark, an audit row was edited with the triggers off — the verifier failed at that row’s own id.
Write-once means write-once. An anchored object’s recorded version cannot be deleted or overwritten — tested with the writer’s own credential and as root.
Even proof has a door. Before anything you approved goes out, you get the full text by email. Exporting the record itself needs your approval, downloads once, and is itself audited.
05 · What it costs to run

Cheap like a tool, capped like a policy.

The bill is a governance decision, not a surprise: hard breakers on spend, on one machine, with the safety lane outside the meter entirely.

£10 / day
hard daily cap
Model-spend caps are breakers, not alerts — when a cap trips, only model work pauses. Safety and orders never stop.
£150 / month
hard monthly cap
In the benchmark week, spend stayed under both caps — 1p a day on the fake provider’s prices, 5p in the month.
1 machine
everything runs on it
No cloud bill for the shop’s brain: the simulators, the record and the models’ runtime all live on one box. Hosting waits for real customers to be approved.

The honest fine print

  • The shop is a reference business on simulators; every order, message and person is made up. Company OS has served no real customer and handled no real payment — by design, built to be proven on simulators first.
  • Every figure here is measured with a fake model provider. Real models are the next step, and the page says so.
  • It is a prototype: refunds, purchase orders, the books, subscriptions and marketing campaigns are out of scope for this version.
