        Step 3 of 6 · The learnings

        What counts, and where it was said.

        A language model on this machine reads the numbered lines and writes down what matters: pains, requests, budgets, objections and decisions. Each one must cite the lines it came from, or it is refused.

        Step 4 of 6 · The grades

        Said is not done.

        A frozen grader scores each learning by what it cost someone to say it, from E0, an inference, to E5, a signed commitment. The same learning gets the same grade every time.

            Threads

            One thread per company. Dormant is computed: an open thread with no call for 90 days. Won and lost are yours to mark; nothing moves a thread for you.

            Step 5 of 6 · The signals

            One call is an anecdote. Three are a pattern.

            Each learning gets a short statement, and the same statement joins the same signal, whichever call it came from. A signal's stage comes from rules over grades, calls and dates, never from the model.

            From calls to signals

            The thicker the line, the higher the grade

            Suggested join

            How a signal earns its stage

            • CandidateOne learning at E2 or above.
            • Emerging3 learnings at E2 or above, from 2 calls, within 90 days.
            • Validated2 learnings at E4 or above, from 2 calls, within 180 days.
            • FadingIts newest support has aged. Nothing is deleted.

            Step 6 of 6 · Your call

            Then you decide, and argue against yourself.

            Open decisions are drafted from what was said. You give each option a probability and say what would prove you wrong. The label says whether the evidence backs you; the model never decides.
