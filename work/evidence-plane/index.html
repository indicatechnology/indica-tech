Value / 01

Calls in. Cited evidence out. Decisions you can defend.

Input

What you give it

  • Recorded calls and meetings, audio or video
  • A consent record for each call: who was told, and when
  • Your calendar, so each call knows its meeting
Output

What comes back

  • Learnings: pains, requests, budgets, objections, decisions
  • Each one cited to the sentence, the speaker and the minute
  • A grade from E0 to E5 for how solid it is
  • Signals, a thread per company, and the decisions waiting for you
Value

What it is worth

  • Check any claim in one click, instead of trusting a summary
  • See what is solid, what is noise and what is fading
  • Decide on evidence, not on the loudest voice in the room
  • Keep it all: your machine, your data, your proof of consent
How you use it / 02

Record as you already do. The rest happens on its own.

01

Record the meeting

Use the recording you already make in Zoom, Teams or Meet, or any audio or video file.

No new habit
02

Let it land in your inbox

Point your recorder's save folder at the inbox folder, drop the file in, or upload it. It is picked up by itself.

No filing
03

Confirm people were told

One short form: who was on the call, and when they were told it was recorded. Where the law asks for consent, record that.

Lawful by design
04

Read the evidence

The call is transcribed, read, cited and graded on its own. Open any claim on its sentence, and file the call under its company.

Answers you can check

Nothing is read before step 03. Withdraw consent later, and that call's evidence leaves every page while the record that you had consent stays.

The grades / 04

Graded by what it cost to say it.

  1. E0Inferred

    The lines do not say it; it is an inference.

  2. E1Vague

    Said, but without specifics.

  3. E2Specific

    Said with a number, a date or a name.

  4. E3Impact

    It states an effect: “this costs us two days a week”.

  5. E4Behaviour

    An action already taken: “we switched vendors in March”.

  6. E5Committed

    A binding commitment, such as a signed order.

How it is held

Each line above is held by a test, not promised in a deck. A result below its bar is reported as a miss, beside the passes, never smoothed over.

The grader is frozen, so a grade means the same thing next month as it does today. A new grader adds its grades beside the old ones; none is rewritten.
