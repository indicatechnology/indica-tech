One Man Army
Selected work / 07 · In-house product

One person runs the company. The system runs the routine.

One Man Army is the operating system for a company run end to end by one accountable person. Rules run the routine, AI agents draft and propose, and the founder makes only the decisions that need judgement. Every action the system takes on a customer, a supplier or a platform passes one controlled gate — and is recorded.

Where it stands, said plainly

The design is complete and has been reviewed twice. The build is real and in progress — twenty-eight of thirty-two work packages are done, and the twenty-ninth is being finished now — but it runs locally, on simulators, with synthetic data. There is no business on it yet, and no figure on this page pretends otherwise: what is measured is the build, and what is a target for the business is labelled a target.

It is shown here because the hard part of this product is the discipline, and the discipline can be shown before the launch: every claim on this page traces to a decision, a requirement or a benchmark in the project's own records.

01 / What it is

Attention runs out before work does.

A company has eight jobs that never stop: the founder's office, growth, customer care, operations, quality and compliance, finance, technology, and its people and partners. One person holding them all fails in three ways — and adding AI agents on their own makes each failure faster.

01

Attention

Every refund, supplier query and advertising change queues behind one inbox. The business grows only as fast as that inbox does.

02

Authority

Tools and agents can act, but nothing decides which actions they may take, up to what limit, and who answers for them.

03

Evidence

When something goes wrong there is no trustworthy record of who decided what, and why. A wrong refund and a missed safety report look the same afterwards.

The answer is a governed system, not a person with a stack of tools. Rules run the routine with no AI at all. Agents draft and propose; checkers on a different provider can only reject. Only a person — or a rule a person signed — approves, and every action passes one gate that re-checks the authority before it runs and records it afterwards.

The design is proven against the hardest case its founder could pick: a regulated UK consumer-health brand sold direct online, where a customer's safety report must reach a person within minutes and every advert must stay inside the claims allowlist. What holds there, the design argues, holds for any retailer.

02 / How it fits the other two

The hub. Two feeders. No side doors.

One Man Army is the third of three systems built here, and the one the others feed. It is the only one that keeps the company's record and the only one that acts. The other two are its memory and its second opinion — joined by narrow contracts that can never make it act.

The three links, and the loop they close

    Evidence reaches the hub read-only: signals, positions and short statements, class P, each with an id, a grade and a hash. A board paper can be reproduced after a re-grade.

  • LINK B

  • VECTORS

    The board's scoring rules are owned by the hub and published as test fixtures. The Board Room product, run for other firms, must keep passing them.

The loop: a partner says something that matters → the evidence plane grades it → it reaches the morning brief as evidence → a decision that needs judgement goes to the board → the founder approves in the hub → the gateway acts → the verdict returns to the evidence plane as an outcome, for calibration only. Each system can be stopped, and the others carry on; the spokes never talk to each other.

03 / The rules that never change

Eight rules, held at every scale.

  1. 01

    Only a person approves. No AI approves anything; agents draft, propose and check. The founder, a delegate, or a rule a person set can authorise an action.

  2. 02

    One gate for every action. Every refund, order, message or advert takes one path, checked against authority and recorded before it runs. Nothing is paid or shipped twice.

  3. 03

    Safety and money never wait on AI. Both paths work with every model switched off.

  4. 04

    The record is the truth. Every event is stored before anything happens; everything else derives from it and can be rebuilt.

  5. 05

    Words are never orders. What a customer, a supplier or a document says is information. It can never instruct the system.

  6. 06

    Every change to the AI is a release. Versioned, tested against agreed cases, approved, and reversible.

  7. 07

    Checkers are independent. They run on a different AI provider from the agents whose work they check, and they report to people, never to agents.

  8. 08

    Attention is budgeted. Each approver's time is a budget the system enforces — and safety work is never held back to save it.

What else was checked, and what missed

Every benchmark that has run, with its number. Intake answers webhooks in a 13 ms p99, and a dropped webhook is found in 210 s against a 300 s bound (MB-01). The safety line handled 100 messages with every provider failing (MB-05). The replay test settled 20 of 20 effects with the executor killed, zero duplicates (MB-04).

Autonomy is earned on counted evidence, not vibes: a class of work is promoted only at exactly 99, 299 or 598 clean reviewed actions by binomial bounds at 95% confidence — the ladder test was verified at the exact boundary, and demotes on a severe error (MB-11).

Claims-safe drafting: 59 of 59 planted unsafe drafts were rejected and 101 of 101 good drafts passed (MB-06); all 31 injection cases ended in a safe outcome (MB-12). The restore drill lost 58.7 s of records against a 15-minute bound, ran in 61.9 s against a 4-hour one, and produced zero duplicates (MB-09). The audit anchor's lag measured 300.19 s against a 360 s limit, and tampering was caught (MB-14).

The misses, on the page: the founder's holdout evaluation set has never been run; the checker model that the live evaluation waits for is chosen in the next phase; one defect is carried — an enrolment token starting with "-" fails about once in 64; and two integration tests flake under full-suite load, recorded as flakes rather than fixed silently. A miss is shown as a miss.

05 / What it is not

The honest limits.

  • It is not a business yet. The design is complete and twice reviewed; the build is 28 of 32 work packages in, running locally on synthetic data. There are no customers, and no launch date is promised.
  • The billion is a stress test, not a forecast. The design is walked to £1B — about 82,192 orders a day, at most one order in 2,000 reaching the founder — to prove it never needs a rebuild. A profitable plateau at £1M–£3M a year counts as success by default.
  • Accountability stays with a person. The law puts it on a named director. The system can delegate work, never the accountability.
  • Some judgements need qualified people. Regulatory sign-off, product safety and tax sit with contracted specialists who approve inside their authority. AI never clears a safety report.
  • Attention is the real ceiling. Growth spends the founder's hours faster than steady running does. The system tracks the hours weekly and forces the decision before they run out: full-time, more help, or a plateau.
  • It is built to be grilled. The next design stage exists to shrink Architecture A — merge parts, cut the founder's upkeep towards two hours a week, and take the open decisions. Nothing here is declared finished.
